# Harness 1.13.0 (contract 1.13.0)

The 5 Whys and the kaizen register (C4), the last phase of the Release Confidence companion.
1.8.0 made a release one command; 1.9.0 to 1.12.0 gave it a score, the PR behind each point, a
history and a place to look. What was missing was the step that makes the rest improve: when the
line stops, or confidence drops, the harness now opens a 5 Whys by itself, answers the first why
from its own trace, and keeps a register of every countermeasure until a release shows it
working. Nothing in it is a break: a consumer written against 1.12.0 keeps working, and no
verdict or exit code changes. It is a **minor** release because it adds a command, two flags, a
directory and a field. The contract changelog entry is in [../contract.md](../contract.md#changes);
the Lean view behind it is [docs/lean.md](../lean.md); the register is
[kaizen/README.md](../../kaizen/README.md).

## What is new

- **`harness why --run <run dir | harness release dir> --finding <id> [--out kaizen/] [--tag T]`**
  writes `kaizen/<date>-<tag>-<finding>.md`. Why 1 is already answered, from data: the finding,
  its artefact, the journeys that reach it, a link to the hunk, the nearest claim and why it did
  not cover, the PR with its files and churn, a first contribution as a fact, and where the glance
  ranked it and what the Reviewer marked. Whys 2 to 5 are blank. The countermeasure line takes one
  of seven kinds. `--finding` is `gate`, `band`, `rollback`, a run rule (`three-declines:rcs`,
  `countermeasures-rising`), a glance item or a hunk id.
- **Started by the harness, not by a person remembering to.** `harness release` writes a stub into
  its own `kaizen/` for a Gate FAIL, a Red band, each run rule firing at the release, and open
  countermeasures only rising, and lists them in the report's "5 Whys (kaizen)" section.
  `harness glance mark --mark escalated` writes one for the item. `post-deploy.yml` puts the
  rollback stub in the rollback issue.
- **`harness why check <file|dir...>`** says whether a filled 5 Whys is ready for the register,
  and exits `1` when it is not. Every answer must be there up to where the chain ends. No answer
  may be only "human error", carelessness or a person's name. The chain must end in a process or
  a tool. There must be exactly one countermeasure kind (a mutant names its path under
  `mutants/`), an owner and a due date. Each rejection gives its Lean reason.
- **The register**, `kaizen/README.md`: the header is written by people. The table is generated
  from the files by **`harness why register [--write]`** and is never edited by hand. It lists the
  trigger, the countermeasure, the owner, the due date, and the release each countermeasure was
  verified closed in. It starts empty: nothing is invented. CI checks every 5 Whys in `kaizen/`
  and fails if the table is out of date.
- **The loop closes on the scoreboard.** Each line records `openCountermeasures`. When the count
  rises three releases running, a run rule fires ("open countermeasures only rising") and the
  harness opens a 5 Whys on the loop itself. `harness release` prints the register's open and
  overdue counts for SOP step 12.

## Kaizen, not blame

The check enforces rules that were only written down before:

| The rule | What `harness why check` does |
| --- | --- |
| Each answer is checkable against an artefact, a PR or a document | Why 1 is written by the harness from the run's own files, with links; people are asked to correct it, never to replace it with a recollection |
| "Human error" is not an answer; it is the prompt for the next why | an answer that is only human error, carelessness, a mistake or "the reviewer missed it" is rejected with that sentence; blame beside a cause that can be checked is kept |
| The register records countermeasures to the system, and "a person was careless" is never one | an answer, or a countermeasure, that is only a person's name is rejected; a first contribution appears as a fact about a PR, and the author is never named |
| The chain ends when the answer is a process or a tool | `Chain ends at` and `Ends in: process \| tool` are required |
| Seven countermeasure kinds, the seven things the team controls | exactly one of mutant · journey · mask review · EARS spec · claim guidance · SOP change · glance rule |
| A new mutant is the best kind | a mutant must name its path under `mutants/`, and the register links it |
| Every 5 Whys has an owner and a due date (SOP step 12) | both are required; the register counts open ones, and overdue ones |

## The companion, 1.8.0 to 1.13.0, in Lean terms

Each phase put one Lean idea into the release, in the order the plan set. Every phase kept the
same guardrail: the Gate wins. No score, trend, glance or 5 Whys is an input to `gate.ts`, and
none of them changes an exit code.

| Version | Phase | Lean idea | What a release gained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.8.0 | One command | Standard work, jidoka | `harness release --candidate <tag>`: SOP steps 2 to 7 as one command that asks nothing, stops the line where it must (`line stopped at <stage>: <why>` and the next standard step) and always leaves a report |
| 1.9.0 | C0 Score | Visual management | the Release Confidence Score after the Gate: eight dimensions, every lost point with its evidence, fixed bands, and "not measured" is never scored 100 |
| 1.10.0 | C1 Changes | Visual management, gemba | `harness changes`: one risk line per PR, every deduction on a file in a PR's diff, a first contribution as a fact, never a person |
| 1.11.0 | C2 Scoreboard | Visual management over time | `scoreboard/releases.jsonl`, append-only, six trend views, run rules that act on trends rather than one bad release, and the harness's own health beside the product's |
| 1.12.0 | C3 SOP and glance | Standard work, gemba | the monorepo's `release/SOP.md`, and at most seven ranked places to look, each linked to the hunk, the claim and the PR, marked verified, disputed or escalated |
| 1.13.0 | C4 5 Whys | Kaizen | a 5 Whys opened by the harness on every trigger, Why 1 answered from data, a check that turns blame into the next why, and a register whose open count is itself watched |

All five Lean ideas are now in the tooling. What is left is evidence from use: the build-order
table in [docs/lean.md](../lean.md) lists, for each phase, what real releases still have to
show before its gate is met.

## Lean in the harness

| Idea | In 1.13.0 |
| --- | --- |
| Jidoka (stop the line) | every stop has a follow-through: a Gate FAIL opens a 5 Whys, so a stop ends in a countermeasure to the system, not only in a new rc |
| Visual management | the register's open and overdue counts are printed after the score, and open countermeasures are on the scoreboard line |
| Standard work | SOP step 12 ("the register reviewed") has a command and a count; the 5 Whys of SOP step 9 (Red or Gate FAIL) is written by `harness release`, and the one of step 11 (a rollback) arrives in the rollback issue |
| Gemba | Why 1 links to the hunk, the claim and the PR; an escalated glance mark becomes a 5 Whys on why the artefacts could not show it |
| **Kaizen** | **live**: from a finding to one countermeasure of seven kinds, owned and dated, verified closed in a named release |

## Behaviour that changes without a flag or field

- `harness release` prints the 5 Whys it opened (or "no trigger fired") and the register line
  after the scoreboard lines. `report.md` and `report.html` list them under "5 Whys (kaizen)",
  where 1.8.0 to 1.12.0 said "not built yet". The score stage's plan title names the stubs.
- `harness glance mark --mark escalated` prints where it opened the 5 Whys, and `--json` adds
  `why`.
- `harness scoreboard append` reads the checkout's `kaizen/`, and the line carries
  `openCountermeasures`.
- `harness scoreboard trends` reports "open countermeasures only rising" from the lines. It stops
  saying the register does not exist, and is not measured only until a line records a count.
